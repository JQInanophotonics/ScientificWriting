# 07 — Style

## Follow Strunk and White

The sentence-level rules in this guide come from William Strunk Jr. and E. B. White, *The Elements of Style*. Read the book.

Apply these rules throughout the paper:

- **Omit needless words.**
- **Use definite, specific, concrete language.**
- **State ideas positively.**
- **Keep related words together.**
- **Use parallel structure for parallel ideas.**
- **Place the important words at the end of the sentence.**
- **Give each paragraph one purpose.**

Readers cannot challenge an argument they cannot follow.

## A scientific exception to active voice

Strunk and White advise writers to use the active voice. This should not be an absolute rule in a scientific paper.

Begin with the setup, system, condition, or information already known to the reader. Move toward the new result. The authors should not automatically become the subject.

Write:

> The Kerr nonlinearity synchronizes the soliton to the reference field.

Do not automatically write:

> We show that the Kerr nonlinearity synchronizes the soliton to the reference field.

Use passive voice when it preserves this order or when the actor does not matter.

> **Begin with the setup. End with the result.**

## Rules from the group's papers

1. **Begin the sentence with the setup or condition, end with the result.**
   The reader reads from left to right and remembers the end of the sentence. Put the conditions (powers, frequencies, the configuration) first, then what is obtained under them. A sentence that starts with the result and trails off into conditions buries the result in the middle of the paragraph.

2. **Make the logic explicit.**
   Each sentence must follow from the previous one, and the reader must see why. Link them with *because*, *since*, *therefore*, *thus*, *as a result*. Two sentences placed side by side without a link leave the reader to guess whether the second is a consequence, a contrast or a new topic. If no link word fits, the sentence is probably in the wrong place.

3. **State what is new against what is known.**
   A result means something only next to what the field already does. Use *unlike*, *although*, *in contrast*, *whereas* to place the new mechanism next to the conventional one, and say precisely where they differ. Saying that a result is "novel" or "unique" does not do this; the comparison does.

4. **Replace qualitative words with numbers.**
   "Much narrower", "large", "good agreement", "significantly improved" carry no information a reviewer can check. Give the value and what it is compared to: the linewidth before and after, the improvement as a factor, the agreement as a deviation. If a number is not available, the claim is not ready to be written.

5. **Make the physics the subject.**
   The subject of the sentence is what the reader should look at: the soliton, the noise, the device, not the authors. "We" is fine when the authors made a choice or performed an action, but not as the default opening. In the group's published papers, only 6 to 17 % of sentences start with "We".

## Be consistent

Use the same terminology, symbols, units, colors, fonts, and visual conventions throughout the paper. Do not replace a precise term with a synonym to avoid repetition. A color, line style, or symbol must retain the same meaning in every figure. The reader should evaluate the science, not decode its presentation.
