# 06 — The conclusion closes the loop and opens

The conclusion mirrors the introduction. The introduction raised a problem. The conclusion answers it, in the same words, then says what the answer now makes possible. Someone who reads only the introduction and the conclusion must see the question and its answer.

## Two formats

- **Letters, short papers and conference abstracts.** One paragraph, sometimes two, starting with "In conclusion". It answers the problem, recaps the results in the order of the Results, and opens. In a conference abstract it is one or two sentences: the answer to the problem, then what it opens.
- **Long articles (Nature, Nature Photonics).** A Discussion. It does not recap the Results. It opens with how the work differs from the conventional approach of the introduction, then argues the limitation that remains and what the work opens.

## Rules

### Close the loop

1. **Answer the problem of the introduction, in its words.** Restate the claim, now as demonstrated.
2. **Take back each limitation the introduction listed, and show it removed,** with the number and the same comparison point.
3. **Recap each result once, in the order of the Results, with its number and its cause.** Letters only.
4. **Return to the stakes of the first paragraph of the introduction:** the same applications, the same analogy.

### Open

5. **State the limitation that remains, and say why it is not fundamental or how it will be removed.**
6. **Open: say what the work now makes possible,** from the nearest step to the farthest, each with the mechanism that enables it.
7. **No new result.** Everything stated was shown in the Results.

## In published papers

**The problem of the introduction, answered in its words (rule 1).**

> However, thus far this model has only demonstrated qualitative agreement in the KIS DC regime, and its second-order temporal derivative---crucial for chaotic DKS---remains experimentally unverified.
>
> In conclusion, our combined experimental and theoretical work quantitatively verifies that the second-order Adler model accurately describes KIS DKS dynamics.
>
> — AC-KIS, PRL 2025

> A natural question that arises is: is it possible to generate PDCSs in foundry-ready, pure-Kerr resonators with bichromatic driving?
>
> We have shown theoretically, numerically, and experimentally that dispersive resonators with a purely Kerr-type $\chi^{(3)}$ nonlinearity can support parametrically driven cavity solitons under conditions of bichromatic driving.
>
> — Parametrically driven pure-Kerr temporal solitons, Nature Photonics 2024

**Each limitation of the introduction, removed (rule 2).** The introduction of the PDCS paper lists three drawbacks of conventional cavity solitons:

> noise on the external CW laser can degrade the coherence of nearby comb lines, removal of the CW background may require careful spectral filtering, whilst applications that require coexistence of distinguishable binary elements are fundamentally beyond reach.

The Discussion takes back all three:

> Moreover, the lack of a dominating CW component at the PDCS carrier frequency alleviates the need for careful spectral shaping, and could result in fundamental advantages to noise characteristics. […] Finally, the fact that PDCSs come in two forms with opposite phase opens the doors to a new range of applications that require a binary degree of freedom, including all-optical random number generation and realisations of coherent optical Ising machines.
>
> — Parametrically driven pure-Kerr temporal solitons, Nature Photonics 2024

> these integrated microcombs fall short in their repetition rate noise performance - primarily due to their poor ability to manage the cavity thermorefractive noise (TRN), which increases inversely with the resonator size.
>
> Our work demonstrates that despite the low modal volume of integrated microcombs, which makes them predisposed to large TRN that can greatly hinder their performance, KIS can enable ultra-low-noise performance […]
>
> — All-optical noise quenching, Optica 2025

**The results in order, with the number (rule 3).** The conclusion of DKS self-balancing (PRA 2026) follows its three Results sections: "In the simplest quadratic-dispersion system […]", "Extending our analysis to systems that include higher-order dispersion […]", "For a quartic-dispersion resonator supporting two DWs […]".

> The repetition rate noise is suppressed by more than four orders of magnitude compared to free-running single pumped-DKS, and is about an order of magnitude lower than the minimum TRN that the singly-pumped DKS could exhibit in our system.
>
> — All-optical noise quenching, Optica 2025

**The stakes of the first paragraph (rule 4).** The introduction of KIS (Nature 2023) names "the power budget […] associated with such active stabilization" as the barrier and draws the mechanical clockwork. The conclusion returns to both:

> Analogous to mechanical clockworks, passive synchronization is enabled by direct coupling to the reference. […] Our work is an important step toward compact optical clocks for fieldable applications in positioning, navigation, and timing, both through decreased power budget and a simplified architecture […]
>
> — KIS, Nature 2023

**The limitation that remains (rule 5).**

> Although detecting our large repetition rate currently requires a phase modulator apparatus for downconversion, this high repetition rate is an integral part of the architecture.
>
> — Self-aligned PDCS, Nature 2026

**What the work opens (rule 6).**

> Moving forward, our self-aligned PDCS enables several integration capabilities, many arising from the stabilized, octave-separated pumps that remain available for further use. For example, the system is highly suitable for integrated SHG of the negative pump for high signal-to-noise ratio CEO detection.
>
> — Self-aligned PDCS, Nature 2026

The Discussion then goes from integrated SHG, to harmonic generation for atomic references, to bandwidths beyond one octave.

## Before submission

Put the introduction and the conclusion side by side and check:

- Does the conclusion answer the problem of the introduction, in the same words?
- Does each limitation listed in the introduction come back, removed?
- Does the conclusion return to the applications of the first paragraph?
- Does it say what the work now makes possible, with the mechanism for each step?
- Is every statement shown in the Results?
