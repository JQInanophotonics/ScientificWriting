# 06 — The conclusion closes the loop and opens

The conclusion mirrors the introduction. The introduction raised a problem. The conclusion answers it, in the same words, then says what the answer now makes possible. Someone who reads only the introduction and the conclusion must see the question and its answer.

## Two formats

- **Letters, short papers and conference abstracts.** One paragraph, sometimes two, starting with "In conclusion". It answers the problem, recaps the results in the order of the Results, and opens. In a conference abstract it is one or two sentences: the answer to the problem, then what it opens.
- **Long articles (Nature, Nature Photonics).** A Discussion. It does not recap the Results. It starts from how the work differs from the conventional approach described in the introduction, then argues the limitation that remains and what the work opens.

## Rules

### Close the loop

1. **Answer the problem of the introduction, in its words.** The introduction ended on what is missing, unverified or impossible. The conclusion opens by stating that it is now done, reusing the same nouns and verbs, so the reader recognizes the question.
2. **Take back each limitation the introduction listed, and show it removed.** If the introduction gave three drawbacks of the conventional approach, the conclusion answers all three, in the same order, with the number and against the same comparison point.
3. **Recap each result once, in the order of the Results, with its number and its cause.** Letters only; a Discussion does not recap. One sentence per result, no new detail.
4. **Return to the stakes of the first paragraph of the introduction.** The applications, or the analogy, that opened the paper come back at the end, now within reach.

### Open

5. **State the limitation that remains, and say why it is not fundamental or how it will be removed.** A reviewer will raise it anyway; the conclusion answers first.
6. **Say what the work now makes possible.** This is the opening of the paper: the next steps it enables, from the nearest to the farthest, each with the mechanism that makes it possible. Not a list of applications without a reason.
7. **No new result.** Everything stated in the conclusion was shown in the Results.

## Before submission

Put the introduction and the conclusion side by side and check:

- Does the conclusion answer the problem of the introduction, in the same words?
- Does each limitation listed in the introduction come back, removed?
- Does the conclusion return to the applications of the first paragraph?
- Does it say what the work now makes possible, with the mechanism for each step?
- Is every statement shown in the Results?
